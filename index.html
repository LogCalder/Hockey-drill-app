Help

Getting started

  1. Add pieces. Drag a player, puck, cone, stick, net or coach from the left panel onto the ice. You can drag as many as you like (double-clicking an item also drops one).
  2. Move things. With the Select tool, click a piece to select it and drag to move it. Press ⌫ to delete.
  3. Draw the action. Pick Skate, Backward, Pass, Dump or Shot and drag on the ice. See Pieces & lines.
  4. Name it. In the right panel give the drill a name, choose Half ice or Full ice, and add notes.
  5. Add more drills with + Half ice / + Full ice in the bottom bar. Each drill is one diagram; click a thumbnail to switch.
  6. Bring it to life with Animate (top bar): see the Animation guide.

Your work is saved in this browser automatically. To keep real files, connect folders: see Saving & folders.

Animation guide

Animation turns your diagram into a moving drill. You attach players and pucks to the lines you drew, put the lines in order, and press play. Playing never changes your drill. It always goes back to how you drew it.

Set it up

  1. Draw your drill: players, puck, nets, and the skate / backward / pass / dump / shot lines.
  2. Click Animate in the top bar. A numbered badge will appear on every line that is set up.
  3. Click a line on the ice. The Animation panel opens on the right.
  4. Pick who moves. Click Pick on the ice, then click the piece:
    • Skate / Backward lines move a player or the coach (you can add several).
    • Pass / Dump / Shot lines move a puck. They can only be attached to a puck.
  5. Set the Step number (and a Sub-step if needed; see below). Lines that stay unattached just stay still.
  6. Press ▶ on the play bar (or the space bar).

Steps and sub-steps

  • Steps are global. Everything in the same step happens at the same time. Step 2 starts when the longest motion in step 1 has finished.
  • Sub-steps belong to one player or puck. If one player has several motions in the same step, give them a, b, c. That player does a, then b, then c, each starting the moment the last one ends. Other players are not held up. Example: a player skates forward (1a) then backward (1b) while everyone else keeps going.
  • The badge shows 1a / 1b only where a player or puck has more than one motion in a step.

Triggers: “start when…”

Inside one step, a motion can wait for something else instead of starting at the beginning. Select the motion and use Starts when:

  • A player reaches a checkpoint: click a spot on the ice (an orange flag appears), then click the player or puck that has to get there. Example: “when X reaches the flag, O starts skating.”
  • Another motion starts / ends: click the other line. A pass, dump or shot sets it off when it is released; a skate sets it off when it finishes.
  • Optionally wait a moment after the trigger (the “then wait” box). Remove a trigger with its ×.

Triggers only work between motions in the same step. Flags show only while you are in Animate mode (not in PDFs or videos). If a flag is never reached, the motion just starts with the step, and the Steps list says so.

How the puck behaves

  • A puck is always held by a player or coach, loose, or in flight. A held puck moves with its holder.
  • A puck starts out held by a player standing right next to it (about 6 ft). To force it, pick the puck under Carrying a puck on a skate line (optional).
  • Pass / Dump / Shot leave when the holder reaches the start of the line. So start the line where the holder will be.
  • A pass is timed to land just before the player it is going to gets there. That player (or coach) takes the puck and carries it from then on.
  • A dump just goes into open space: nobody receives it. A shot ends where its line ends.
  • A loose puck stays where it landed until a player skates over it in a later step, who then picks it up.
  • These rules only work inside a step. If nobody is there during that step, the pass, dump or shot still happens in its step; it never waits for something in a later step.
  • Each motion has a Speed slider (0.5× to 2×) if you want it slower or faster.

The play bar and Steps list

  • ▶ / ⏸ play and pause (space bar). ■ stops and takes you back to editing; while previewing, editing is locked.
  • Drag the slider to scrub. The numbers on it mark where each step starts. Choose 0.5×, 1× or 2× speed.
  • The Steps panel (top of the right column) writes out what happens in each step. The current step is highlighted while it plays, and clicking a step jumps to it.
  • Export video records the animation as an MP4 into your Exports folder (see Exporting).

Example: a quick rush

  1. Put F with a puck right beside it, C near the slot, a net, and a G.
  2. Draw a Skate line from F up the ice. In Animate mode select it, Pick F, Step 1.
  3. Draw a Pass from the end of F's skate to C. Select it, Pick the puck, Step 1. It leaves when F gets to its start and arrives just before C.
  4. Draw a Shot from C to the net. Pick the puck, Step 2. It goes once C has the puck.
  5. Press ▶. Check the Steps list to read it back.

If something looks wrong

  • Nothing moves: the line has no player/puck attached (no badge). Select it and pick one. The Steps list warns about unattached lines.
  • A pass happens too early or from the wrong place: a pass starts when its holder reaches its start, so draw the start where the holder will be, and end it where the receiver will be.
  • The puck slides oddly at a hand-off: check that the pass or shot line starts near the player who should have the puck.
  • Something should wait for something else: put it in the next step (or a later sub-step for the same player).
  • Can't edit: you are previewing. Press ■.

Saving & folders

  • Automatic: your work is always kept in this browser, for this web address. That is a convenience copy, not a backup.
  • Drills folder (top bar): choose a folder once. Every drill is then saved there automatically as its own file (name.drill.json).
  • Exports folder (top bar): choose where PDFs and videos go. If you skip it, they download normally.
  • After you restart Chrome the button says Reconnect: click it once to allow saving again. Chrome requires that.
  • Open… adds drills from your Drills folder. Import file… (in that dialog) reads a drill file someone sent you. Share a drill by sending its .drill.json.
  • Safe by design: the app never deletes or moves your files, and never overwrites a file that belongs to a different drill (it saves as name (2)). Renaming a drill leaves the old file behind, so tidy up in Finder. Removing a drill only removes it from the project.
  • Folder saving and MP4 video need Chrome on a computer. Other browsers download files instead.

Exporting

PDF (US Letter)

  • Click Export PDF…. Choose this drill or all drills, and 1, 2 or 4 drills per page (more pages are added automatically).
  • Drills on the same page share one scale, so pieces stay the same size.
  • Tick names and notes to print them under each drill, and the step-by-step list to print what happens in an animated drill.

Video (MP4)

  • Click Export video (top bar or play bar) on a drill that has an animation. It plays and records in real time, so a 10-second drill takes about 12 seconds.
  • The video shows the ice and the moving pieces. It is saved to your Exports folder as name animation date.mp4.
